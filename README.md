This data derives from the written work "An Historical Account of the Black Empire of Hayti" by Marcus Rainsford. Rainsford was a soldier who served the British Army in the British West Indies. Rainsford documented his personal firsthand account of the 1799 Haitian Revolution. In this document, Rainsford highlights the deeds of the Revolutionary leader Toussaint Louverture and the French colonial brutality. In addition, he challenges the negative, racist narratives Europeans held of the black-slave revolt. This dataset represents the historical account of a White, British sympathizer towards the Haitian Revolution. However, this dataset is not free from the patriarchal norms of the late 18th century. In his historical account, Rainsford only mentions women when it relates to a male's standing or as a form of evidence of French brutality. Though he does praise the female population of Haiti, subverting common European narratives, it is an overgeneralization of the victimization and glorification of Haitian women, ignoring wartime contributions such as engaging in combat and aiding in nursing. A row represents a paragraph from Rainsford's text that contains information about women and how they were viewed by Rainsford during this revolutionary period.
